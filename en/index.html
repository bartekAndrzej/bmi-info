What is BMI?

Body mass index (BMI) is a simple measure of how your weight relates to your height. It gives a quick check on whether your weight falls in the range considered healthy for adults. Doctors and researchers use it as a first screening tool, not as a full health assessment.

How to calculate BMI

Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Example: someone who weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75), about 22.9. In imperial units the formula is BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)².

Limitations of BMI

BMI does not measure body composition. Muscular people, such as athletes, can have a high BMI while being very fit. In older adults, muscle loss can be hidden by a normal BMI. BMI also does not show where fat is stored, which affects health risk. For a fuller picture, combine it with waist measurement and medical checks.

Questions and answers

What is a healthy BMI?

The World Health Organization treats a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 as a healthy weight for adults.

Is BMI an accurate measure of health?

It is a simple screening tool. It does not account for body composition, so it can be misleading for muscular people and older adults.

Does BMI work for children?

Not in the same way. For children and teenagers, BMI is compared with age- and sex-specific percentile charts. This calculator is designed for adults.
